# Porsche 968 3.0L Swap

## Baseline status

The 968 3.0L is the Porsche-native control case for the project: how much performance can be gained while remaining
within the original Porsche transaxle engine family?

Porsche lists the 968 at 3.0L, 240 PS and 305 Nm; US references commonly list 236 hp and 225 lb-ft [S004][S005].
Factory-service-manual-derived data gives a dry engine weight of **172 kg / 379 lb**, only about 6 kg more than the
944 2.5 dry figure commonly quoted from Porsche workshop data [S006][S002].

## Mechanical compatibility

Long-running 944/968 conversions show that the engine is physically compatible with the chassis architecture.
On an S2, builders describe the swap as mechanically close to bolt-in while retaining S2 bellhousing, flywheel,
torque tube and transaxle components [S025].

For earlier 944s, the mechanical fit remains favorable but the wiring, flywheel/bellhousing selection, and ancillary work
increase substantially.

## Required donor content

Documented conversions identify the following as important:
- complete 968 engine
- engine harness
- 968 DME
- MAF
- sensors and associated intake hardware
- appropriate 944S/S2 or 968-related flywheel/bellhousing choices depending conversion strategy
- fuel/cooling/exhaust adaptations [S025]

For a conversion, a complete donor package is significantly more valuable than an isolated long block.

## Electrical integration

The later the 944, the easier the integration tends to be.

The original conversion notes document a key difference: the 968 DME requires switched 12 V at **DME pin 27**, supplied through
the 14-pin diagnostic/alarm connector path. A 2016 follow-up thread expanded this into full pinout/change-chart work [S025A][S036].

This is particularly relevant to the project's early 1985 chassis, where connector architecture differs more substantially than
on a late S/S2 chassis.

## Transmission considerations

A 968 engine can be mechanically installed while retaining S2 driveline components. Older conversion discussions generally favor
an S2/Turbo-class transaxle over a base NA gearbox when the full 3.0L output is used.

The 968 six-speed is not automatically the best choice for an early chassis because speedometer integration and installation
details differ. This is a Porsche-native swap, but not necessarily a "drop the complete 968 drivetrain into any 944" operation.

## Strengths

- Porsche engine architecture
- close physical compatibility
- modest dry-weight increase
- 236 hp / 225 lb-ft stock output
- factory-like service layout and character
- no fundamental custom-engine-mount/bellhousing development program required

## Weaknesses

- engines and complete donor packages are scarce
- early-chassis wiring burden is significant
- 30+ year-old engines may require expensive cylinder-head/Variocam/bottom-end work
- donor and rebuild economics can exceed the cost of a mass-market engine plus swap hardware

## Current maturity assessment

**Maturity: High mechanically; low availability/economics.**

The September 2026 sweep found no new commercial 968-to-944 kit ecosystem; the useful knowledge remains builder documentation
and Porsche-family parts compatibility rather than a contemporary vendor package.
