# Project K244 — 2025-2026 Honda K24 / Porsche 944 Case Study

Sources: S016, S031, S032.

## Why this build matters

The Drive's Project K244 is currently the clearest public case study of a K24 installation that attempts to keep the Porsche
944 rear-transaxle architecture rather than redesigning the entire drivetrain.

It is valuable precisely because the project did **not** go smoothly.

## Starting premise

The 2025 project used:
- a Honda K24
- an untested 944-specific overseas swap kit
- original Porsche transaxle concept
- later turbocharging

The Drive explicitly described the kit as obscure/untested and noted that established companies such as KPower support general
RWD K swaps but not the Porsche 944 specifically [S031][S032].

## Problems documented during the project

Across the series and final September 2026 report:
- adapter/driveline integration was difficult,
- the engine required teardown,
- at least two bent valves were found,
- gearbox/interface work was required,
- clutch hydraulics required correction,
- wiring required outside help,
- turbo/intercooler packaging added substantial custom work,
- specialists at Fulltime Fabrication ultimately helped sort the car.

The final article states that virtually every major system—from engine to gearbox to hydraulics—needed attention before the car
would drive [S016].

## Result

By September 8, 2026:
- engine ran,
- car moved under its own power,
- turbo system was operational,
- dyno output was nearly **300 whp**.

This is strong feasibility evidence.

## Engineering interpretation

The correct conclusion is **not** "K24 does not work."

The correct conclusion is:
- K24 fits,
- K24 can retain the Porsche transaxle architecture,
- K24 can make useful turbo power,
- the presently documented 944-specific hardware path requires significant debugging and fabrication.

A second independent build using a well-documented adapter/bellhousing, clutch stack, oil pan, and mount package would materially
raise the maturity rating.
