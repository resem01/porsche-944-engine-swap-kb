# Nissan VK56 5.6L V8 — Feasibility Study

Status: **provisional feasibility-study engine**.

The September 2026 sweep still did not identify a completed Porsche 944/VK56 installation or 944-specific commercial kit.
Current evidence therefore supports retaining the VK56 as an explicit engineering-feasibility study rather than promoting it
to the primary proven-swap group.

## Why investigate it

Nissan's factory specifications describe the longitudinal 5,552 cc VK56DE as a DOHC 32-valve aluminum-block/aluminum-head V8
producing **317 hp and 385 lb-ft** in 2009 Titan form [S023].

Its strong naturally aspirated torque, aluminum construction, low donor cost in some markets, and growing manual-transmission
adapter aftermarket make it likely to recur as a proposed 944 engine.

## Initial packaging evidence

A builder physically measured a non-VVL VK56 at approximately [S024]:
- oil pan to intake top: **33 in**
- rear of block to front of intake: **30 in**
- overall width: **30 in**
- Titan intake height: **10 in**
- intake top to bottom of crank balancer: **25 in**
- balancer bottom to oil-pan bottom: **8 in**

The same builder notes that the lower sheet-metal pan can potentially be shortened with pickup modification, so the 33-in
overall height is not necessarily an immutable installed height.

These are bounding-box measurements, not common-datum 944 measurements.

## Current aftermarket evidence

C3 Performance sells VK56DE/VK56VD to CD009/JK/VQ manual-transmission adapter hardware using 3D-scan-derived geometry [S026].

That proves a modern manual-transmission swap ecosystem exists around the VK, but it does **not** solve:
- Porsche torque-tube attachment
- clutch stack height
- pilot-bearing position
- 944 starter packaging
- oil pan/crossmember geometry

## Known engine-family considerations

Community VK swap experience outside the 944 identifies:
- stock exhaust manifolds as a known weak point,
- timing-chain-guide concerns under sustained hard/high-rpm use,
- significant opportunity to reduce sump depth by modifying the lower pan/pickup [S024].

Those observations are useful for feasibility planning but are not 944-specific validation.

## Critical 944 questions

1. Can the DOHC head width coexist with brake booster, steering shaft and rails?
2. Can hood clearance be achieved without unacceptable sump/crossmember compromise?
3. Is a custom rear/shallower sump practical?
4. What is dressed and swap-ready mass?
5. Can a Porsche torque-tube bellhousing be packaged without excessive adapter stack length?
6. Can A/C and power steering survive the installation?
7. Is early port-injected VK56DE materially simpler than VK56VD for control integration?
8. Does 385+ lb-ft make a Turbo/951 transaxle marginal for the intended use?
9. Does the VK56 provide enough benefit over an LS to justify much more packaging development?

## Current assessment

**Remain feasibility study.**

A negative result is still valuable. If common-datum measurement shows that the engine cannot meet hood/steering/crossmember
constraints without major chassis surgery, documenting that conclusion will prevent repeated speculative work.
