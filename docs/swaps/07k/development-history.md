# 07K Swap Development History

This chronology preserves engineering changes that explain the current conversion. It is not intended to reproduce every forum post.

## 2014 — concept phase

The Rennlist thread began as an investigation of the 2.5L 07K as an alternative to the aging Porsche M44/951 engine.

Initial attractions:
- same nominal 2.5L displacement
- compact fore-aft package
- inexpensive donor engines
- modern 20-valve head
- VVT
- large turbo-performance potential
- replaceable iron-cylinder block architecture

Early dimensions and performance claims were exploratory and should not automatically be treated as final measurements.

## 2014-2017 — bellhousing investigation

Concepts included:
- 931 bellhousing
- Audi 01E bellhousing
- cut/boxed Porsche 944 bellhousing
- adapter plate

The rear 07K timing-chain housing repeatedly interfered with available bellhousing shapes.

Result:
- move toward a purpose-built 07K-to-944 torque-tube bellhousing.

## 2018 — purpose-built driveline interface

QuickTime/NineX development merged the general concepts of:
- VW/Audi bellhousing pattern
- Porsche torque-tube interface
- hydraulic concentric release bearing
- reverse-mounted 02M starter

The engine angle evolved from about 15° to about 25° so that hood clearance could be achieved while keeping the Porsche crank/torque-tube centerline at stock height.

This period also established the 02M flywheel/pressure-plate + 944 Turbo clutch-disc architecture.

## 2018-2019 — packaging systems developed together

Development shifted to:
- engine mounts
- modified oil pan
- intake manifold
- power steering
- turbo placement
- cooling

Important rejected/modified concepts:
- stock 07K intake with factory booster
- simple unmodified transverse oil pan
- early 15° engine angle
- completely fabricated pan as the primary production approach

## 2019 — first commercial kit structure

By August 2019 the first post listed:
- base mounts/valve cover/oil-filter block/power-steering kit
- separate bellhousing
- SPEC flywheel/clutch
- oil pan under development
- cast intake under development
- ECU/harness under development
- rear coolant flange and thermostat housing still under development

This is a historical kit snapshot, **not** the current parts list.

## 2020 — system completion and running cars

Major milestones:
- production oil-pan assemblies
- production intake
- rear coolant flange
- thermostat solution
- PE harness/ECU
- working clutch hydraulics
- factory gauge integration
- detailed cooling routing
- turbo development car on dyno

Page 131 is especially useful for:
- slave-cylinder bolts
- clutch bleeding
- pre-installation engine assembly sequence
- wiring-before-intake advice

## 2021 — retrospective reliability information

By August 2021 the developers described the turbo car as sorted.

Two notable development defects were documented:
- faulty oil-filter-block check valve
- early PE harness coil-resistor specification

Both were resolved.

## 2023-2024 — builder-kit resale and additional builds

The thread documents older-generation Boost Brothers kits changing hands. These posts are useful because they reveal what early kits did **not** include.

A later independent builder reported first start in a 1989 Turbo chassis in 2024.

## 2024-2026 — current commercial maturity

The current Boost Brothers catalog is much more complete than the 2019 kit:
- Phase 1 and Phase 2 packages
- full kit
- oil pan
- intake
- bellhousing
- clutch/flywheel
- rear coolant flange
- thermostat adapter
- power-steering relocation
- valve/timing covers
- oil-filter block
- Haltech ECU/harness

Current full-kit price at the baseline date:
- **$6,295**

Current release-bearing requirement:
- GM 24237569 or FTE 1102235

Current ECU:
- Haltech Elite 750 package

These current specifications supersede the 2019 thread's original pricing and several original part numbers.

## Why retain the history

The engineering history explains:
- why the engine is tilted 25°
- why a dedicated bellhousing exists
- why the stock intake was abandoned
- why the oil pan is a machined-core design
- why the accessory drive uses a 1.8T power-steering solution
- why some part numbers found in old forum posts are now wrong for a new kit
