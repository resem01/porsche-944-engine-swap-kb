# VW/Audi 1.8T — Documented 944/924-Family Builds

This file records representative Motor Werks Racing builds as evidence of conversion maturity. Output figures are vendor-reported.

## Gulf Tribute — 1988 944

Source: [S029](../../SOURCE_INDEX.md)

- 1988 Porsche 944 chassis
- Audi 1.8T 20V
- forged rods, JE pistons
- AEB head
- K04 Sport turbo
- 315 hp / 365 lb-ft
- 7,000-rpm limiter
- Maxx standalone ECU
- 986 cable-shift 5-speed
- rear-mounted alternator
- 2,350-lb completed track-car weight

Technical significance: demonstrates a lower/mid-power developed build using a 986 gearbox rather than the factory 944 transaxle.

## FATurbo Express — 1983 944

Source: [S044](../../SOURCE_INDEX.md)

- 1983 Porsche 944 chassis
- Audi 1.8T 20V
- forged rods
- AEB big-valve head
- Garrett GT2860RS
- 345 hp / 300 lb-ft
- Porsche 986 gearbox
- 997 shifter/cable arrangement
- rear-mounted alternator
- manual brakes

Technical significance: another independent MWR track-car implementation around the same conversion family.

## Rothman's / GTP-style build — 1987 944 chassis

Source: [S030](../../SOURCE_INDEX.md)

- 1987 Porsche 944 chassis
- Audi 1.8T 20V
- forged rods / developed head
- BorgWarner 6758 EFR
- 450 hp / 395 lb-ft
- 8,000-rpm limiter
- Porsche 987 gearbox
- custom axles
- 2,050-lb completed weight

Technical significance: establishes that the MWR architecture scales well beyond the NA-transaxle power level, but does so by
changing the rear gearbox/axle system.

## What these builds do — and do not — prove

They prove:
- the 1.8T physically packages in 944/924-family chassis,
- MWR has repeated the conversion,
- current vendor hardware is not based on a single prototype,
- both original-Porsche-transaxle and later Cayman/Boxster gearbox strategies exist.

They do **not** prove:
- every quoted power figure independently,
- that the standard DIY kit reproduces the full track-car specification,
- that factory HVAC/brake assist can be retained,
- that a base NA transaxle is safe at the 400+ hp level.
