# Evidence and Provenance Standard

Every important technical claim should be traceable to its source.

## Evidence classes

**P — Primary / manufacturer**  
Factory workshop manuals, OEM technical publications, engineering drawings, official specifications.

**M — Physical measurement**  
A documented physical measurement or scale weight with configuration and datum sufficiently defined
to reproduce or evaluate it.

**B — Builder-documented**  
Detailed first-hand builder information supported by photographs, measurements, part numbers,
dyno data, or repeatable installation results.

**V — Vendor**  
Vendor or kit-manufacturer documentation. Useful, but commercial claims remain attributed to the vendor.

**A — Anecdotal**  
Forum or social-media report without enough supporting detail for independent verification.

**I — Inference**  
A calculation or engineering inference derived from identified source data. The inputs and method
must be shown.

## Status

Each technical item may also be marked:
- Current
- Superseded
- Disputed
- Unverified
- Needs measurement

## Rules

- Never convert an estimate into a measured value.
- Never compare weights unless the attached-component definition is known.
- Preserve superseded part numbers and designs when they explain the development history.
- Prefer common dimensional datums: bellhousing/torque-tube interface, crankshaft centerline,
  front crank pulley, sump low point, cylinder-head/valve-cover high point.
- When two credible sources disagree, record the disagreement.
