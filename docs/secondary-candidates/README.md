# Secondary Engine Candidates

These engines are retained because real 944 work exists or because they recur frequently in swap discussions. They remain outside
the primary matrix because current 944-specific support, packaging efficiency, or repeatability is weaker than the primary candidates.

| Engine family | Documented 944 evidence | Why it remains secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Toyota 2JZ | Multiple builds exist. One 2020 car used a CD009 plus 350Z rear subframe/diff and reportedly made 530 whp; another builder reported retaining the Porsche torque tube/transaxle [S039][S043]. | Long/heavy inline-six package; most builds are highly custom and may abandon the Porsche transaxle architecture. |
| Nissan RB-series | A documented RB25DET NEO-swapped 944 ("Porzilla944") was running by 2022 [S038]. | Long I6 package, donor cost/scarcity, little standardized 944 hardware. |
| Volkswagen VR6 | A 2009 project fabricated a modified VW bellhousing and adapter to the Porsche torque tube and reached test-fit work [S041]. | Starter/tunnel and height/orientation problems; no mature current kit identified. |
| Toyota 1UZ | A detailed 2014 build used an XSchop torque-tube adapter, T56 slave, custom clutch combination, hydroboost, and chassis-rail notch [S040]. | Wide DOHC package; substantial custom work; historical adapter availability uncertain. |
| Ford Coyote | Builders report at least one 944 installation; a 2025 comparison describes service clearance in finger-width terms [S042]. | Very wide DOHC heads; poor service space compared with LS; no mature 944 kit identified. |
| Buick 3800 | Multiple historical 944 conversions are referenced in community discussions. | Aging donor base and little current standardized hardware. |
| Chevrolet 4.3 V6 | Can conceptually leverage SBC-family bellhousing/swap hardware. | Similar fabrication burden to a V8 for much less output; weak current 944-specific support. |

## Interpretation

These are **not rejected engines**.

The 2JZ, RB25 and 1UZ in particular should no longer be described as merely hypothetical: completed or substantially developed
944 installations exist. What keeps them secondary is lack of a repeatable contemporary ecosystem—not lack of physical possibility.

Promotion into the primary comparison requires one or more of:
- strong current 944-specific kit/support
- multiple independently documented completed builds
- compelling common-datum packaging/weight advantage
- a clearly repeatable torque-tube/drivetrain solution
- renewed donor availability/economics

The Nissan VK56 is treated separately as a formal feasibility study because its growing swap popularity makes it likely to recur
as a proposed 944 powerplant even though no completed 944 installation has yet been found.
